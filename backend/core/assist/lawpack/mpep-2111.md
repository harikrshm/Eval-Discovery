title: Claim interpretation

MPEP 2111 explains how patent examiners read claims. During examination, a claim is given its broadest reasonable interpretation consistent with the specification, as a person skilled in the field would read it. The specification is the main guide to what the words mean, and an inventor can act as their own dictionary by giving a term a special definition there.

Claim words are normally given their ordinary and customary meaning unless the specification says otherwise. Examiners read claims in light of the specification, but they generally do not add limitations that appear only in the described embodiments into the claim itself.

This is how a claim is read during examination, so it is background on how the claim was examined, not the rule for reading an issued patent. Courts and the Patent Trial and Appeal Board in review trials read claims by the specification and prosecution history under their own rules, which often give a narrower reading. This summary does not give a reading of any claim on the page.
