id: mpep-2173
title: Claims must be definite
source: MPEP 2173
status: draft
---
MPEP 2173 covers the requirement that claims be definite, that is, that they set out and circumscribe the invention with a reasonable degree of precision and particularity. Examiners ask whether a skilled reader, looking at the claim in light of the specification, would understand what is and is not covered.

The section stresses that breadth is not the same as indefiniteness: a claim can be very broad and still be perfectly clear. A claim that uses a term of degree such as "about", "substantially" or "relatively" is not automatically indefinite; the question is whether the specification or the field gives the reader enough guidance to understand it.

When the scope of a claim cannot be determined, the examiner may reject it as indefinite so that the applicant can clarify the wording. This summary describes the examination practice only and does not say whether any claim on the page is definite.
