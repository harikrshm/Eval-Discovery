title: Rights granted and patent term
source: 35 U.S.C. 154
status: draft
---
Section 154 describes what a US patent gives its owner and for how long. The patent grants the right to exclude others from making, using, offering for sale or selling the claimed invention in the United States, or importing it into the United States.

For patents filed on or after June 8, 1995, the term generally ends 20 years from the earliest US application the patent claims the benefit of, not counting a provisional application. The term can be lengthened to make up for certain delays at the patent office (called patent term adjustment). Other rules, such as the payment of maintenance fees, can end a patent earlier than that date.

The expiry date shown on a patent page is typically a calculation or estimate, not an official legal ruling. Patents filed before June 8, 1995 follow a different term rule. This summary does not state when any patent expires.
