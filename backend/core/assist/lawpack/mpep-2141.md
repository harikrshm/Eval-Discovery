title: Examination guidelines for obviousness
source: MPEP 2141
status: draft
---
MPEP 2141 describes how patent examiners analyze obviousness under section 103. The analysis rests on a set of factual questions: what the scope and content of the prior art are, how the claimed invention differs from it, how much skill a typical person in the field has, and whether objective evidence such as commercial success, long-felt but unmet need or failure of others points toward non-obviousness.

The guidelines call for a flexible, fact-based approach that considers the claimed invention as a whole and the prior art as a whole. The examiner must give a reason why a skilled person would modify or combine the prior art to arrive at the claim; a reason is required, but it need not follow a rigid teaching-suggestion-motivation formula. Hindsight, meaning using the inventor's own claim as a roadmap to pick pieces of prior art, is to be avoided.

This describes the examination approach in general. It does not decide whether any claim is obvious.
