title: Patent-eligible subject matter and utility
source: 35 U.S.C. 101
status: draft
---
Section 101 says who can get a US patent at all. A patent may be granted for a new and useful process, machine, manufacture or composition of matter, or a new and useful improvement of one of those. Two ideas sit inside that sentence. First, the invention must fall into one of those categories. Courts have long held that laws of nature, natural phenomena and abstract ideas are not eligible by themselves, so a claim that is really just one of those can be rejected or later challenged under this section. Second, the invention must be useful, meaning it has some real, workable purpose.

This is a threshold question that is separate from novelty (section 102) and obviousness (section 103): an invention can be new and still fail eligibility, and the reverse. Whether a particular claim is eligible is a fact-specific legal judgment that this summary does not make.
