title: Transitional phrases: comprising vs consisting of
source: MPEP 2111.03
status: draft
---
The transitional phrase of a claim is the word or words joining its preamble to the list of elements, and it affects how wide the claim is.

"Comprising" is inclusive, or open-ended. A claim to "a device comprising A and B" is still met by a device that also has C. Words such as "including", "containing" and "characterized by" are generally read the same way.

"Consisting of" is closed. It excludes any element, step or ingredient that the claim does not name. Unrelated impurities are not necessarily counted.

"Consisting essentially of" sits between the two: it allows additional items only if they do not materially change the basic and novel characteristics of the invention.

So a "comprising" claim usually reaches further than a "consisting of" claim with the same list of elements. This summary explains the vocabulary only and does not say what any claim on the page covers.
