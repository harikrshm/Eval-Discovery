Section 271 defines patent infringement. Under 271(a), whoever without authority makes, uses, offers to sell or sells a patented invention in the United States, or imports it into the United States, while the patent is in force, infringes the patent. Section 271(b) covers actively and knowingly inducing someone else to infringe, and 271(c) covers selling, offering to sell or importing a component that is especially made or adapted for use in a patented invention, knowing it was made or adapted for infringing use, and that is not a common article with substantial other non-infringing uses.

Courts decide infringement by comparing the accused product or process with the words of the claims, one claim at a time. Literal infringement of a claim generally requires that every limitation of that claim is present. There is also a court-developed route called the doctrine of equivalents. Only claims that are valid and in force count.

This summary explains the idea in general terms. It does not say whether anyone infringes, and it is not advice about making, selling or using anything.
