---
id: usc-112f
title: Means-plus-function claim elements
source: 35 U.S.C. 112(f)
status: draft
---
Section 112(f) lets a claim element be written as a "means" or a "step" for performing a stated function, without spelling out the structure, material or acts that perform it. The trade-off is how such an element is read: it covers the corresponding structure, material or acts described in the specification for that function, and equivalents of them, rather than every possible way of performing the function.

So for an element such as "a means for fastening", the scope depends on what fastening structure the specification actually describes. Whether an element is treated this way is a matter of claim interpretation. Wording such as "means for" usually triggers it. A stand-in such as "module for" or "mechanism for" can too, if it names a function without enough structure to do it. Wording that recites a clear structure usually does not. If the specification describes no structure for the function, the element can be indefinite.

Older patents cite this as the sixth paragraph of section 112. This summary explains the rule only; it does not say how any specific claim element should be read.
