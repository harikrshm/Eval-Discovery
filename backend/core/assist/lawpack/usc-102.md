title: Novelty and prior art

Section 102 is the novelty rule. In general, a claim cannot be patented if the claimed invention was already patented, described in a printed publication, in public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the application's effective filing date. A claim can also fail if the invention was described in an earlier-filed US patent or published application that names a different inventor.

There is a limited grace period: in many cases, a disclosure made by the inventor (or by someone who got it from the inventor) one year or less before the effective filing date is not counted against the claim.

A claim is usually called anticipated when one earlier item shows, expressly or inherently, every element of the claim, arranged as the claim requires. If the earlier item is missing even one element, section 102 alone does not apply (section 103 may still be relevant).

These are the rules for the current first-inventor-to-file system, which applies to applications with an effective filing date on or after March 16, 2013. Older patents were judged under different, date-of-invention rules.
