title: Obviousness
source: 35 U.S.C. 103
status: draft
---
Section 103 says a claim cannot be patented if the differences between the claimed invention and the prior art are such that the invention as a whole would have been obvious, before the effective filing date, to a person having ordinary skill in the technology. Unlike novelty, obviousness does not need one earlier item that shows everything: several prior art references can be combined if there is a reason a skilled person would do so.

Examiners and courts usually work through a set of questions: what the prior art teaches, how the claim differs from it, how skilled a typical person in the field is, and whether there is real-world evidence pointing toward non-obviousness, such as commercial success or a long-felt need that others failed to solve.

Obviousness is judged claim by claim and depends on the specific prior art and the facts. This summary explains the idea only; it does not decide whether any claim is obvious.
