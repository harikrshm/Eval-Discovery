---
id: usc-112b
title: Claims must be definite
source: 35 U.S.C. 112(b)
status: draft
---
Section 112(b) requires the claims, which come at the end of the patent, to particularly point out and distinctly claim what the inventor regards as the invention. In plain terms the claims must be clear enough that people can tell where the protected territory begins and ends.

A claim that is unclear on that point can be rejected or challenged as indefinite. In court, the usual test is whether, read in light of the specification and the prosecution history, the claim tells a person skilled in the field the scope of the invention with reasonable certainty. During examination, the Patent Office applies its own, more lenient test for ambiguity. A claim is not indefinite just because it is broad, and a word such as "about" or "substantially" is not automatically unclear.

Older patents cite the same rule as the second paragraph of section 112. This summary explains the rule only; it does not say whether any claim on this page is definite.
